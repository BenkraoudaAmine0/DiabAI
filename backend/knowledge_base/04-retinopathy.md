# Diabetic Retinopathy

## What it is

Diabetic retinopathy is damage to the small blood vessels of the retina
caused by prolonged hyperglycaemia. It is a leading cause of preventable
blindness in working-age adults. Early stages are usually asymptomatic,
which is why annual retinal fundus photography is recommended for everyone
with diabetes from the time of diagnosis of type 2 diabetes, and within five
years of diagnosis of type 1 diabetes.

## The five severity grades

Retinopathy is graded from retinal fundus photographs:

1. **No DR** — no diabetic retinal lesions visible.
2. **Mild non-proliferative DR** — microaneurysms only; tiny dot
   haemorrhages may be present.
3. **Moderate non-proliferative DR** — more than microaneurysms: retinal
   haemorrhages, hard exudates, cotton-wool spots, venous beading begins.
4. **Severe non-proliferative DR** — extensive haemorrhages (more than 20 in
   each quadrant), intraretinal microvascular abnormalities, or venous
   beading in two or more quadrants — high risk of progression.
5. **Proliferative DR** — new abnormal blood vessels grow on the retina or
   optic disc (neovascularisation); these vessels can bleed into the vitreous
   and cause tractional retinal detachment — sight-threatening emergency.

Diabetic macular oedema — fluid leaking into the central macula — can occur
at any stage and is the main cause of vision loss.

## Symptoms

Often none until late: blurred or fluctuating vision, floaters, dark or empty
areas in the visual field, difficulty seeing at night, impaired colour
vision. Sudden floaters or vision loss need urgent ophthalmological review.

## Screening and management

Screening uses fundus photography interpreted by AI grading systems or
specialists. Management: strict glycaemic and blood pressure control slows
progression. Severe non-proliferative and proliferative disease are treated
with pan-retinal laser photocoagulation or intravitreal anti-VEGF injections;
macular oedema with anti-VEGF or focal laser. Early detection dramatically
improves outcomes — over 90% of severe vision loss can be prevented with
timely treatment.
