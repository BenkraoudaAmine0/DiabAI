# HbA1c and Blood Glucose — Diagnostic Ranges

## Glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c)

HbA1c reflects the average blood glucose over the previous 2 to 3 months. It
is expressed as a percentage and does not require fasting.

- **Normal**: HbA1c below 5.7%
- **Prediabetes**: HbA1c between 5.7% and 6.4%
- **Diabetes**: HbA1c of 6.5% or higher (confirmed by a second test)

Prediabetes is a warning state: blood glucose is above normal but not yet in
the diabetic range. Without intervention, many people with prediabetes
progress to type 2 diabetes, but lifestyle changes can prevent or delay it.

## Fasting plasma glucose

Measured after at least 8 hours without caloric intake:

- **Normal**: fasting glucose below 100 mg/dL (5.6 mmol/L)
- **Prediabetes (impaired fasting glucose)**: 100 to 125 mg/dL
  (5.6–6.9 mmol/L)
- **Diabetes**: 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) or higher

## Oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT)

After 75 g of glucose: values below 140 mg/dL at 2 hours are normal;
140–199 mg/dL indicate prediabetes (impaired glucose tolerance); 200 mg/dL or
higher indicate diabetes.

## Random plasma glucose

A random glucose of 200 mg/dL or higher in a patient with classic symptoms of
hyperglycaemia supports a diagnosis of diabetes.

## Targets for people living with diabetes

A common HbA1c treatment target is below 7% for most non-pregnant adults,
individualised by age, duration of diabetes and overall health. Very tight
control may be inappropriate for frail or elderly patients. Continuous or
regular self-monitoring of blood glucose complements the quarterly to
biannual HbA1c check.

## Combined interpretation

Both HbA1c and glucose are used together. A patient may have an HbA1c in the
prediabetes range (5.7–6.4%) together with fasting glucose in the 100–125
mg/dL range — this combination is the classic prediabetic profile. In the
DiabAI screening tool, a model output of "Non-Diabetic" combined with HbA1c
between 5.7 and 6.5% and glucose between 100 and 126 mg/dL is reported as
"Prediabetic" for this reason.
