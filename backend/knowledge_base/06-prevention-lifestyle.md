# Preventing Type 2 Diabetes — Lifestyle Guidance

## The evidence

Randomised trials (including the Diabetes Prevention Program) show that
lifestyle modification in people with prediabetes reduces progression to
type 2 diabetes by around 58% — more effective than medication alone in the
same populations. Even modest weight loss produces disproportionate benefit.

## Weight

Losing 5–10% of body weight substantially improves insulin sensitivity.
For a person weighing 90 kg, that means 4.5–9 kg. Weight around the abdomen
(visceral fat) drives insulin resistance more than fat elsewhere, so waist
circumference matters (risk increases above 94 cm in men and 80 cm in women).

## Physical activity

Aim for at least 150 minutes per week of moderate aerobic activity — brisk
walking, cycling, swimming — spread over at least 3 days, plus 2 sessions of
resistance training. Muscle is the body's main glucose sink; contracting
muscles take up glucose without insulin. Interrupting prolonged sitting every
30 minutes also improves glucose profiles.

## Diet

- Prefer whole grains, legumes, vegetables, fruit, nuts and olive-oil-based
  patterns (Mediterranean-style eating).
- Limit sugar-sweetened beverages — the single strongest dietary risk factor.
- Choose water or unsweetened beverages as the default.
- Reduce refined carbohydrates, processed meats, and foods high in saturated
  and trans fats.
- Control portions; energy balance drives weight change.
- Moderate alcohol; do not smoke — smoking doubles cardiovascular risk in
  diabetes.

## Sleep and stress

Chronic short sleep and untreated obstructive sleep apnoea worsen insulin
resistance. Aim for 7–8 hours of sleep. Sustained stress raises cortisol,
which elevates glucose.

## Monitoring

People with prediabetes should have HbA1c or fasting glucose rechecked at
least annually, and watch for the visible warning signs: acanthosis
nigricans on the neck, recurrent skin infections, or excessive thirst and
urination.

## Small consistent changes win

150 minutes of walking a week, one fewer sugary drink a day, and 5% weight
loss are realistic targets that measurably prevent diabetes.
