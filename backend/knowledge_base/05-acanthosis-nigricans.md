# Acanthosis Nigricans — Neck Sign of Insulin Resistance

## What it looks like

Acanthosis nigricans (AN) is a skin condition presenting as symmetric, dark,
velvety thickening of body folds — most visibly on the back and sides of the
neck, but also the armpits, groin and knuckles. The skin may feel velvety or
slightly raised and may itch. It is not an infection and is not contagious.

## Why it matters

Acanthosis nigricans is the most visible cutaneous marker of **insulin
resistance** and hyperinsulinaemia. Excess insulin stimulates skin
keratinocytes and fibroblasts, producing the characteristic thickened,
pigmented patches. Because insulin resistance is the driving mechanism of
type 2 diabetes, AN is widely used as an early, cost-free screening sign:
spotting it on the neck can flag prediabetes years before glucose values
become abnormal, especially in young and overweight individuals.

## Who develops it

It is common in people with obesity, polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), and
in families with type 2 diabetes, and it is more frequent in darker skin
phototypes. In children and adolescents, AN plus obesity strongly predicts
future type 2 diabetes.

## What to do when detected

- Screen for insulin resistance and dysglycaemia: fasting glucose, HbA1c,
  and where indicated an oral glucose tolerance test.
- Address reversible drivers: weight reduction, dietary change and regular
  physical activity frequently lighten the lesions and improve insulin
  sensitivity.
- Rarely, sudden widespread AN reflects an internal malignancy
  (stomach cancer classically) or medication effects — rapid onset without
  obesity warrants medical evaluation.

## Key message

Dark velvety skin on the neck is a visible early warning of insulin
resistance. It is a reason to test glucose and HbA1c and to start lifestyle
prevention — not merely a cosmetic issue.
